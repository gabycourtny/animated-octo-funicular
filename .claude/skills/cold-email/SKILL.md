# Cold Email Writing Guide

This document establishes best practices for B2B cold outreach. Here are the key takeaways:

**Core Philosophy**: Write as a thoughtful peer would — conversational and human-centered, not template-driven. As the guide states, emails should "sound like they came from a sharp, thoughtful human — not a sales machine."

**Essential Elements**:
- Understand the recipient's role, desired outcome, value proposition, proof points, and any relevant research signals before writing
- Lead with the prospect's world, not yours — their challenges should dominate the narrative
- Personalization must connect directly to the problem or it's just decoration

**Email Structure**: Choose from frameworks like observation→problem→proof→ask, or trigger→insight→ask. There's flexibility here — the framework serves the message, not vice versa.

**Subject Lines**: Keep them brief (2-4 words), lowercase, and internal-sounding. They should look like colleague-to-colleague communication, not marketing pitches.

**Follow-Up Sequences**: Use 3-5 touches with increasing gaps. Each follow-up introduces a fresh angle or proof point — "just checking in" wastes the opportunity.

**What Kills Performance**: Avoid stale openings like "I hope this email finds you well," corporate jargon, feature lists, and requests for 30-minute meetings on first contact.

The guide emphasizes ruthless editing: every sentence must advance toward a reply.
